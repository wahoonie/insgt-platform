# AB 723 — Digitally Altered Image Disclosure

## What the Law Says

California Assembly Bill 723 (BPC § 10140.8), effective January 1, 2026, requires real estate brokers, salespersons, or anyone acting on their behalf to disclose when marketing images have been digitally altered and to provide access to the original, unaltered versions.

The law creates two categories of photo editing:

### Does NOT Require Disclosure

Standard photo editing adjustments that do not change the representation of the real property:

- Lighting, brightness, exposure correction
- White balance, color correction
- Sharpening, angle correction, straightening
- Cropping
- HDR tone mapping (combining exposure brackets)

### Requires Disclosure

Alterations that add, remove, or change elements of the real property or elements visible from the property:

- Sky replacement
- Object removal (utility poles, wires, neighboring structures)
- Virtual staging (adding furniture, appliances, decor)
- Adding grass, pool water, fireplace effects
- Removing clutter, equipment, imperfections
- Changing paint colors, flooring, landscaping, facades
- Altering views through windows or exterior views

### Disclosure Requirements

When a digitally altered image is used, the agent must:

1. Include a "reasonably conspicuous" statement near the image disclosing the alteration
2. Provide a link, URL, or QR code to the original unaltered image
3. If published on a website the agent controls, either include the unaltered image directly or link to it

## How This Affects InsightPhotos

### Our Role

We are "a person acting on behalf of" the real estate agent. Our customers (agents) are ultimately responsible for compliance, but we provide the tools and media that make compliance easy or hard. Our goal is to make compliance effortless.

### What We Process

Every photo we deliver goes through standard processing — exposure correction, white balance, HDR tone mapping, color correction. None of this triggers AB 723.

Some photos receive additional enhancements that do trigger disclosure requirements — primarily sky replacement, but also object removal, virtual staging, and other material alterations. These are the photos that need an associated unaltered version.

### The Photo / UnalteredPhoto Relationship

In our data model, a `Photo` represents the final delivered image. When that photo has undergone alterations that trigger AB 723, an `UnalteredPhoto` record is created and associated with it. This is the version before any disclosure-triggering enhancements, but after standard processing (exposure, white balance, HDR).

Key distinction: the UnalteredPhoto is NOT the raw camera file. It is a fully processed photo that simply hasn't had material alterations applied. It represents what the property actually looks like, professionally photographed.

The presence or absence of an UnalteredPhoto on a Photo record is the single flag that drives compliance logic throughout the entire system.

### Where Compliance Surfaces

The AB 723 relationship between Photo and UnalteredPhoto affects nearly every client-facing feature:

**Downloads (client-app, ops)**
Clients need to download the right files for their use case. Someone posting to MLS on a compliant platform needs both versions with proper labeling. Someone posting to social media needs the disclosure-ready package. The download interface must make it clear which files require disclosures and which don't.

Current terminology in the download UI:
- "No Disclosures Required" — the unaltered versions that can be used anywhere without disclosure
- "MLS Ready - Disclosures Included" — the enhanced versions packaged with their unaltered counterparts for MLS compliance

**Galleries (galleries)**
Public shareable galleries that agents send to clients, stagers, or other parties. When photos have unaltered versions, the gallery must present them in a way that supports the agent's disclosure obligations.

**Disclosure Gallery (disclosure-gallery)**
A dedicated public page providing the unaltered images for a shoot. This serves as the "publicly accessible internet website" that agents can link to or generate QR codes for, satisfying the law's requirement for access to original images.

**Virtual Tour (insgt-virtual-tour)**
Tours displayed on MLS platforms need to handle altered images with appropriate disclosures since these are published on platforms the agent may or may not control.

### Shoot-Level Compliance Status

Not every shoot contains altered photos. An order's compliance status is determined by whether any of its photos have associated UnalteredPhotos. This status drives which download options and UI elements are shown to the client.

If a shoot has zero photos with unaltered versions, the compliance UI elements are unnecessary and should be hidden to avoid confusing agents who don't need to think about disclosures for that particular order.

Legacy shoots that were taken before the law are not-compliant, so the UI changes to indicate its compliance state is unknown. Clients can pay to have a legacy shoot brought into compliance.

## Industry Context

### MLS Handling

Different MLS systems are implementing AB 723 differently:

- **CRMLS** (California's largest): Requires altered photos to be labeled and original images included adjacent to the altered version in the listing
- **SFAR**: Added a toggle during upload for agents to mark images as digitally altered, which adds a watermark
- **SDMLS**: Enforcing requirements across listings and downstream data feeds (IDX, VOW, APIs, syndication)

This inconsistency means our clients face different requirements depending on which MLS they use. Our job is to provide the raw materials (both photo versions, clear labeling) so agents can comply regardless of their MLS's specific implementation.

### What Agents Care About

Agents want to:
1. Not get in trouble
2. Not think about it
3. Have it be fast

They do not care about the technical distinction between HDR tone mapping and sky replacement. They care about: "Can I use this photo as-is, or do I need to do something extra?" Our interface language and workflows should answer that question instantly.

### No Established Penalties Yet

As of early 2026, there are no specific fines defined for non-compliance. Violations fall under the Department of Real Estate's disciplinary process and could also trigger exposure under California's consumer protection and false advertising statutes. The practical risk for agents is reputational and legal liability, not a specific fine schedule. This means agents vary widely in how seriously they take compliance — our system must support the cautious agents without burdening the less concerned ones.

## Key Principles for Development

1. **The UnalteredPhoto relationship is the source of truth** — all compliance logic keys off whether a Photo has an associated UnalteredPhoto, not a separate flag or status field
2. **Language matters** — avoid terms like "original" and "edited" which are ambiguous. Use disclosure-focused language that tells agents what they need to do, not how the photo was processed
3. **Progressive disclosure** — hide compliance UI when it's not relevant (shoots with no altered photos) but make it prominent when it is
4. **Support all compliance paths** — agents may need to download just unaltered versions, just altered versions with disclosures, or complete packages. Don't force a single workflow
5. **We provide tools, not legal advice** — our interface facilitates compliance but we never tell agents they are or aren't compliant. That's between them, their broker, and their MLS
